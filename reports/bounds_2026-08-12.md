# How far from the limit are we? A bound calculation, and the data needed to finish it

Motivation: after sixteen experiments, only one class of change (adding cluster-level information) has ever moved the metric. Before running a seventeenth, the right question is not "what else can we try" but "how much is left to take". This note computes what is computable, states what is not, and identifies the one sample that would settle it.

## 1. The bound for a fixed linear estimator (valid, stable)

For an unbiased estimator that is a fixed linear combination of cell energies, w'x, the Gauss-Markov theorem gives the minimum variance

    var(E_hat) >= 1 / (f' C^-1 f)

with f the mean response profile (energy deposited per cell per unit photon energy, measured on the clean sample) and C the covariance of the residual x - E*f measured on min-bias. Estimated at ring level (5 dimensions, stable at our sample sizes, condition numbers 14-93):

| region | n (min-bias) | bound sigma_E [GeV] | bound at the low-E edge | **measured (our model)** | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 mm | 8367 | 14.2 | 0.31 (at 46 GeV) | **0.168** | **1.8x better** |
| 30 mm | 13981 | 9.5 | 0.29 (at 33 GeV) | **0.095** | **3.1x better** |
| 40 mm | 20876 | 3.7 | 0.20 (at 19 GeV) | **0.061** | **3.3x better** |
| 60 mm | 24943 | 2.2 | 0.18 (at 12 GeV) | **0.050** | **3.6x better** |

**The model beats the optimal fixed linear estimator by a factor 1.8 to 3.6.** That is not a contradiction of the theorem: our estimator is neither linear nor fixed. It infers the sub-cell impact position and the local pileup level from the observed pattern and effectively chooses different weights for every event, which a fixed vector w cannot do.

This is the theoretical explanation for a long run of negative results. Optimal-variance weighting, two-term optimal weighting, full-covariance GLS, ring-rho subtraction and the signed gate are all attempts to improve a *fixed linear* rule, and the table says the learned conditional estimator is already 1.8-3.6x past the best any such rule can do. Measured confirmations: fixed optimal weights were worse than the learned gate (0.206 vs 0.165 at ring level), and the signed gate — which lets the network express the GLS solution's negative outer weights — changed nothing (+0.0007).

Practical consequence: **stop proposing better weighting schemes.** The remaining headroom is not there.

## 2. The bound we could not compute, and why

The number that would answer "is 0.05 reachable in the inner low-energy bins" is the bound for a *conditional* estimator: one that knows the shower shape and impact position and only has to fight pileup. That requires the covariance of the pileup contribution alone, C_pu.

Attempted as C_pu = C_minbias - C_clean, both estimated from data. It failed, in two different ways, and both failures are instructive:

- At cell level (81 dimensions) the difference has near-zero and negative eigenvalues. Regularising by clipping them to a small floor produces directions with almost no apparent pileup variance, which the inverse then weights enormously, giving a nonsense bound of 0.065 GeV — a hundred times better than measured performance.
- At ring level, projecting onto the positive eigendirections instead discards information and inflates the bound to 42 GeV at 15 mm, i.e. **worse than the all-noise bound of 14 GeV**, which is logically impossible since pileup is a subset of the noise. The 60 mm region returned 30 GeV, plainly unphysical.

Difference-of-covariances is not a usable estimator at these sample sizes, and no amount of shrinkage fixes the fact that the two matrices come from different event samples. This is reported rather than quietly dropped because the intermediate numbers looked attractive: 0.0014 relative resolution would have been a spectacular and completely wrong claim.

## 3. The one sample that settles it

A **paired overlay sample**: the same photons, reconstructed twice, once without and once with minimum-bias overlay. That single addition makes everything above directly measurable rather than inferred:

- C_pu becomes a within-event difference, not a difference of two population covariances, so the conditional bound is computable and stable.
- The true per-cell pileup contribution becomes known, so the emergent gate's correlation with the photon fraction can be validated per cell rather than in aggregate. **Done 2026-08-16, and the aggregate-era figure did not survive: per cell the gate correlates r = 0.211, not 0.92** (`reports/gatesup_2026-08-16.md`).
- The containment fraction and the contamination fraction become separable, which the current data cannot do — the profile audit shows the 9x9 window holds 99% of the photon (clean) but only 27% of the cluster at 15 mm (min-bias), and only paired events show which part of the difference matters.
- It also gives the denominator for a fair statement of the pileup penalty, currently quoted across two independently generated samples.

This is now the top ask for Felipe, ahead of the design a/b/c values.

## 4. What this implies for the 0.05 target

- 60 mm low-E is already at 0.0503 and 40 mm low-E at 0.0605, both within reach of the calibration work still untried.
- 30 mm low-E at 0.0948 needs a factor 1.9. The linear bound is 0.29 there, so nothing forbids it; but our estimator is already 3.1x past the linear optimum, and the remaining route is more information, not better weighting.
- 15 mm low-E at 0.1683 needs a factor 3.4, in a region where the photon is a third of the window energy and the window sees 27% of the cluster. No calculation we can currently perform says whether that is allowed. Anyone claiming it is, or is not, without the paired sample is guessing.

## 5. Method notes for reproduction

Profiles f from `.scratch/cache/clean-aux_100.pkl`, residual covariances from `.scratch/cache/minbias_94.pkl`, rings defined as max(|di|, |dj|), cells above the 2.49 MeV threshold inside the 9x9 window. Bounds use `numpy.linalg.pinv` on the ring covariance; condition numbers are reported alongside so the reader can judge stability. Measured resolutions are 5-seed `SubNetW4CleanAuxExDnQdEma` values from `scripts/score_regions.py --bin all`.
