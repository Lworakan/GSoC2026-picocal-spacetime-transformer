# Plan: how far below 0.0388 we can get without new data

2026-08-23. Target set by Worakan: $\sigma_{\rm eff} = 0.036$, ideally $0.035$,
with no additional simulated sample.

## Verdict first

**0.036 is reachable and I would bet on it. 0.035 is not, on current evidence.**

The reason is arithmetic, not pessimism. Two levers remain and both are already
measured:

| lever | measured size | status |
|---|---|---|
| the two-stage window | $-2.6\%$ (0.0388 → 0.0378, dev split, $P=1.00$) | done, but only on the development split |
| training on 90% instead of 70% of the sample | $(0.9/0.7)^{-0.18} = -4.4\%$ from the measured scaling curve | **never collected** |
| ensemble diversity across configurations | 0.0378 → 0.0375 pooling 25 members | partially collected, free to extend |
| perfect centring (bound) | $-4\%$ total, most already taken | closed |
| per-cell separation (bound) | observables-gate within 10–12% of truth-gate | closed |

$0.0388 \times 0.974 \times 0.956 \approx 0.0361$. That is the whole plan: the
best configuration has never been run under the protocol that also gives it the
larger training set. Everything beyond that is worth $1$–$2\%$, which lands near
$0.0355$ at best and does not reliably clear $0.035$.

## The runs, in order, with a gate after each

**Phase 0 — free, no GPU (today).** Pool every existing member across the four
families already on disk into one prediction set and score it. Currently 25
members give 0.0375 on the development split. Confirms the pooling gain is
stable before paying for anything.

**Phase 1 — 10 folds, 1 seed, two-stage configuration (~3 GPU-hours).**
`--fold k --rc-mode pred --rc-pred .scratch/pointer.pkl` for $k = 0 \dots 9$.
The pointer must be regenerated per fold from members that did not see that
fold's test events, otherwise the centre leaks — one extra `gen_pointer` run per
fold, cheap.
*Gate:* per-fold single members should land near $0.0373$ (from 0.0389 at 70%
data). If they land at 0.0389 the scaling curve does not transfer to this axis
and the plan stops here, having cost 3 hours.

**Phase 2 — seeds 1–4 for all folds (~12 GPU-hours).** Gives 5-seed ensembles
per fold, the honest counterpart of the development-split 0.0378.
*Expected:* $\mathbf{0.0360-0.0365}$, fully out-of-sample. This is the number
that would replace 0.0388 on the front page.

**Phase 3 — diversity, not more seeds (~4 GPU-hours).** One `--aux` and one
`--distill` member per fold, pooled with the two-stage members. Pooling across
configurations beat pooling across seeds by 0.0003 on the development split.
*Expected:* a further $-0.0003$ to $-0.0005$ → $\approx 0.0357$.

**Phase 4 — only if Phase 2 overshoots (~2 GPU-hours).** `--rc-regions 0 1 2 3`
to stop the pointer hurting at 120 mm (0.0581 vs 0.0555 there), and snapshot
ensembling from cyclic restarts, which buys ensemble diversity at one training's
cost and has never been tried.

**Total: about 20 GPU-hours on an H100**, in four billable chunks with a stop
condition after each.

## What is deliberately not in the plan

- More architecture search. 148 configurations, ten encoder families, and the
  two measured bounds above say the remaining headroom there is $1$–$2\%$.
- Augmentation of any kind. D4 was neutral, overlay was neutral, and the
  scaling curve's gain comes from pileup configurations that transforms cannot
  manufacture.
- Quality cuts. They make $\sigma_{\rm eff}$ worse by construction.

## Honesty note for the paper

If Phase 2 lands at 0.036, the correct sentence is that the improvement came
from *the protocol training on more of the sample plus the two-stage window* —
not from a new idea. The 70%→90% part is a data effect measured on our own
curve, and should be labelled that way rather than presented as a modelling
gain.
