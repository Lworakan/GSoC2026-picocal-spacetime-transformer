## The lever that is not yet priced, and that needs nobody's permission

`--gatesup` teaches the per-cell gate the **true photon fraction**, which is
known only on the synthetic overlay sample. That sample is **9,717 events
against 72,554 real ones** — the supervision that bought 2.3% is reaching
13% of the training set.

We generate the overlay ourselves (`scripts/make_overlay.py`). More
supervision is therefore available without any new production, which is the
one lever in this project that does not depend on the collaboration.

What is *not* known is whether the benefit scales with overlay volume or
saturates. The risk is real and measurable: overlay events are separable from
real minimum bias at AUC 0.93 (0.714 once two event-level columns are
dropped), so a large overlay fraction could teach the model overlay
statistics. The `--synaux` control bounds this — adding overlay events
*without* the gate loss is neutral (12/400) at the current volume — but that
control has to be repeated at each new volume, not assumed.

## Sequence, with the decision gate for each step

**1. Finish the fold repair.** `Rr01` and `Ac`, folds 5–9, are training now.
Recompute the five-member headline and every per-bin table from it.
*Gate:* if the ensemble does not improve by roughly the 2% the single seed
showed, the single-seed result was not representative and the fold story
needs re-examining before anything is published.
*Cost:* running; ~40 minutes.

**2. Confirm gate supervision at the production configuration.**
`Gs20Rc`/`Gs50Rc`, three seeds, dev split — checkpoints from the interrupted
run survive at epoch 79.
*Gate:* it must beat its matched control in ≥ 380/400 paired resamples. The
W4-era result is dev-split and one era old; two previous dev-split winners in
this project failed under cross-validation.
*Cost:* ~1 hour.

**3. Cross-validate it.** Ten folds, the protocol the headline uses.
*Gate:* the same 380/400 bar. This is not optional — it is the step that
killed the two-stage window after it read 400/400 on the development split.
*Cost:* ~4 GPU-hours.

**4. Scale the overlay.** 9,717 → 30k → 50k, with a `--synaux` control at each
volume so the gain is attributed to supervision and not to the extra events.
*Gate:* the gain must grow with volume. If it is flat between 10k and 30k,
the lever is saturated and step 5 is the only route left.
*Cost:* overlay generation is CPU; ~2 GPU-hours per volume.

**5. Whatever remains.** If steps 1–4 land at 0.0368 and the overlay lever
saturates, the residual is 1.32× training data — a far smaller request than
the 3× currently in `data_request_2026-08-23.md`, which was scaled for 0.033.
That document should be rewritten to give the mentors a menu: 1.3× buys
0.035, 3× buys 0.033.

## What this plan does not promise

Steps 1 and 2 are repairs and confirmations of things already measured; they
are likely. Step 3 is where this project has been wrong twice before, and a
failure there removes the largest single lever. Step 4 is genuinely unknown —
there is no measurement of how gate supervision scales with overlay volume,
and the domain-gap risk grows with it.

**0.035 is reachable on the measured path only if step 3 holds and step 4
does not saturate.** If step 3 fails, the honest ceiling is about 0.0376 and
0.035 needs 1.5× data. Both outcomes are worth reporting; only one of them is
worth celebrating.
