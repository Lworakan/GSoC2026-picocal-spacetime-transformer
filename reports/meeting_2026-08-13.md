## 3. Why the inner regions are hard

`scripts/fit_resolution.py` fits sigma(E) = sqrt((a/sqrt(E))^2 + (b/E)^2 + c^2) per region, with per-bin statistical errors and reported parameter uncertainties:

| region | a | b [GeV] | c | E where sigma = 0.05 | chi2/ndf |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15mm | 0.000 | 2.65 +- 0.41 | 0.000 | 53.1 GeV | **23** |
| 30mm | 0.000 | 1.61 +- 0.16 | 0.016 | 33.9 GeV | 3.9 |
| 40mm | 0.135 | 0.46 +- 0.06 | 0.000 | 13.6 GeV | 2.3 |
| 60mm | 0.078 | 0.27 +- 0.04 | 0.013 | 7.1 GeV | 1.6 |
| 120mm | 0.004 | 0.25 +- 0.08 | 0.026 | 5.7 GeV | 1.0 |

Read this carefully, because the honest reading is more interesting than the naive one:

- The noise term dominates the inner regions: b is 2.65 and 1.61 GeV there against 0.25-0.46 GeV outside. At low energy the relative resolution is essentially b/E, which is why those bins look bad while the same regions at high energy are at target (0.023-0.031 everywhere).
- **In the 15mm region the parametrisation does not actually describe the data** (chi2/ndf = 23), and a and c sit on the fit boundary, so their zero values are an artefact of the bound rather than a measurement. With a and c frozen the noise term is 2.62 +- 0.09. Any claim about that region's shape needs a better model or more energy bins; b there is indicative, not measured to better than ~15%.
- **The noise term is not primarily pileup.** For pileup, theory gives b proportional to sigma*sqrt(area) (arXiv:0912.4926), so shrinking 9x9 to 5x5 should scale b by 0.56. We measured b getting 14-32% *worse*. Combined with the earlier nb16 finding that the residual correlates with containment at 0.977, the noise term is **containment-dominated**. That explains why everything aimed at pileup — ring-rho subtraction, timing gates, fixed variance-optimal weights — did nothing, and why the one change that worked was the containment block, which measures exactly the quantity being lost.

So the route to a lower low-energy resolution is better containment estimation, not better pileup rejection. Reweighting cells inside a fixed window has a published ceiling of 10-15% (Naylor 1998 MNRAS 296:339; CMS multifit arXiv:2006.14359; matched-filter studies arXiv:2204.13780), consistent with the 10% we measured for a covariance-weighted ring estimator. Factor-two class gains in the literature come only from shower **attribution** (ClusTEX arXiv:2603.18172: 0.80 vs 6.39 GeV on overlapping photons) or **adaptive thresholding** (SoftKiller arXiv:1407.0408: 20% on pT). A realistic target for the inner low-energy bins is a factor 1.5-1.8, not 2.7.

**Asks**: the design a/b/c values for PicoCal per region, so measured can be compared against design; and guidance on whether a 4- or 5-parameter form is used for the inner regions, given that the standard three terms fail there.

## 4. What did not work, and why it is informative

Seven architecture variants (geometric attention bias, query pooling, explicit time gate, D4 train-time augmentation, physical-coordinate tokens, pileup-density conditioning, and combinations) all landed within 0.0409-0.0426 of the champion's 0.0409 — no aggregate movement at all. Additional negative results:

- **Larger window (13x13)**: aggregate flat. b grows with the area over which pileup is integrated.
- **Smaller window (5x5)**: b got *worse* by 14-32%, contradicting a naive sqrt(area) expectation — containment loss dominates.
- **Ring-rho median-density subtraction**: 15mm low-E worse by +0.0099. Subtracting the mean pileup does not reduce its fluctuation.
- **Explicit time gate, and resolution-weighted time pull on the 9x9 window**: no gain (time pull helped only on the inferior 5x5 window).

The pattern across everything tried: changes that **add information** moved the metric; changes that **rearrange computation** did not. That is the main methodological finding of the past week.

## 5. Timing

Timing is used in all models (per-cell front/back times plus validity flags). Measured per-cell timing resolution from our own data, versus cell energy: 0.756 ns at 2-10 MeV, 0.263 ns at 100-300 MeV, and **0.038 ns above 3 GeV** — a factor 20 spread, which means a cell's time is only meaningful if the cell is energetic. Under pileup every bin degrades by roughly 1.7-2x.

**What timing is worth, measured.** `--no-time` zeroes the four timing channels (front time, back time, and their two validity flags) and changes nothing else — same architecture, same features, same schedule, same seeds. Champion configuration, 2 seeds each:

| bin | with timing | without timing | timing gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15mm low-E | 0.1817 | 0.2559 | **-0.0742 (29%)** |
| 30mm low-E | 0.1123 | 0.1837 | **-0.0714 (39%)** |
| 15mm all | 0.0660 | 0.0797 | -0.0137 |
| 30mm all | 0.0588 | 0.0775 | -0.0187 |
| 40mm all | 0.0361 | 0.0431 | -0.0070 |
| 60mm all | 0.0341 | 0.0383 | -0.0042 |
| 120mm all | 0.0366 | 0.0456 | -0.0090 |
| **aggregate** | **0.0411** | **0.0508** | **-0.0097 (19%)** |

Timing improves every bin, and it helps most exactly where the problem is: 29% and 39% in the two weak low-energy inner bins. Without it the model lands at 0.0508, i.e. roughly where the clean-sample models of the earlier era sat — **timing is what makes the minimum-bias performance possible at all**.

This also corrects an impression from the failed timing experiments earlier in the week. The explicit time gate and the resolution-weighted time pull added nothing, but that means the *existing* encoding already extracts most of the available timing information — not that timing is unimportant. The ablation says the opposite: it is the largest single contributor measured in this project.

The identical ablation on the **new best** configuration (ExDn), seed 0 against seed 0, reproduces it:

| bin | with timing | without timing | timing gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15mm low-E | 0.1628 | 0.2144 | -0.0516 (24%) |
| 30mm low-E | 0.1039 | 0.1634 | **-0.0595 (36%)** |
| 15mm all | 0.0547 | 0.0796 | -0.0249 |
| 30mm all | 0.0499 | 0.0691 | -0.0192 |
| **aggregate** | **0.0399** | **0.0496** | **-0.0097 (20%)** |

Two independent base models, same conclusion: timing is worth about 20% on the aggregate and 24-39% in the weak low-energy inner bins. It is the largest single contributor measured anywhere in this project, and it is what allows a minimum-bias model to reach the resolution the earlier clean-sample models had.
