1. One real file: 1,791 clusters, 139,298 informative cells; cells/cluster p50 = 68, p99 = 183,
   max = 207 — consistent with the Week-1 all-file numbers (p50 ≈ 71). Comfortable sequence
   lengths for a transformer (smoke script).
2. Isolated photons in one file: 35% — consistent with the Week-1 67% cluster-sharing figure
   (≈33% isolated). Confirms the isolation helper and the sharing rate on fresh code.
3. End-to-end on GPU: a 32-cluster batch pads to `[32, 159, 6]`, the mask marks exactly 2,617
   padding positions (matches `B·Lmax − Σ n_cells`), and `SpaceTimeTransformer.embed` runs on

4. Split with seed 0 on one file: train/val/test = 1,449 / 200 / 142 clusters, zero group overlap
   between any two splits (sibling-leakage check passes).

## Surprises / deviations from the plan

- The synthetic time test forced a clean rule for the median-centring: a cell with a garbage time
  contributes neither to the per-cluster median nor a NaN to the network — it gets `t_centered = 0`
  and `t_valid = 0`. That keeps the "time missing" information without ever feeding a NaN.
- `np.log` on a torch tensor raised a NumPy-2.0 deprecation warning in one test; cast to float
  first. No effect on the library.

## Technique research (representation + deployment)

To make sure I understand *what* we are building and *why* it would work in the real PicoCal,
not just as a paper, I did a literature pass and wrote it up in
[`docs/research-notes/2026-week2-representation-and-techniques.md`](../research-notes/2026-week2-representation-and-techniques.md).
Citations checked against arXiv this week. The short version:

- Our cluster is a **point cloud with physics** — the standard modern view (ParticleNet
  arXiv:1902.08570, Particle Transformer arXiv:2202.03772). The 6 features in `dataset.py` are the
  per-point "position + value + depth + time"; nothing wasted.
- The "space-time positional encoding" is a **multi-axis Fourier/RoPE encoding of (x, y, depth, t)**
  with a per-axis frequency scale (Fourier features arXiv:2006.10739, RoFormer arXiv:2104.09864).
  Treating ~15 ps time as a coordinate is the novelty hook — to confirm vs literature and mentors.
- The "graph clustering" baseline is a real family: **GravNet** (arXiv:1902.07987) + **Object
  Condensation** (arXiv:2002.03605), used end-to-end for CMS HGCal pile-up. A 2025 CLAS12 paper
  (arXiv:2503.11277) already pairs GravNet-as-PE with a Transformer — close to our idea.
- With ~200k labels, **supervised end-to-end wins**; keep PCA/UMAP only to visualise the embedding,
  and inject physics shower features (W₀ centroid, depth fraction, moments) as auxiliary inputs.
- **Deployment bar:** the model only earns its place if it uses depth + timing to reject
  out-of-time pile-up (the notebook-02 minbias problem), in a form small/deterministic enough to
  export to ONNX/SOFIE for HLT2 (LHCb Upgrade II TDR CERN-LHCC-2021-012; Allen GPU HLT1
  arXiv:1912.09161; transformers-on-FPGA via hls4ml arXiv:2409.05207). At ~100 cells per cluster,
  exact softmax + FlashAttention beats linear/kernel attention — so I will not over-claim "O(N)".

## Proof-of-concept notebook (03)

`notebooks/03_poc_spacetime_transformer.ipynb` puts the techniques on the real data, end to end
on the GPU: point-cloud input via `dataset.py`, a space-time Fourier positional encoding of
(x, y, depth, time), a DeepSets baseline and a masked transformer (exact softmax / SDPA), the
transformer predicting a residual on top of the calibrated-sum baseline, energy-balanced
training, and a fair val-calibrated evaluation. 20 files, isolated photons, one seed — a POC,
not a measurement.

What it shows (stable across reruns):

- The learned models **roughly halve the robust σ at low energy (1–10 GeV)** vs the calibrated
  sum — the regime where the Week-1 contamination floor dominates. That is where ML helps.
- The **calibrated sum still wins at high energy** (a few %); the small POC models add noise
  there. A POC limitation (model size, data, training), not a wall.
- The **timing ablation is a null on isolated photons**, as expected — no pile-up means timing
  carries little energy information. The real timing test is the minbias sample (notebook 02),
  over several seeds. This is the next experiment.
- PCA of the transformer's embedding is **energy-ordered** (PC1 tracks ln E) — the learned
  representation is sensible; PCA stays a diagnostic, supervised training is the tool at ~12k
  labels.

Deliberate choices recorded: exact softmax + FlashAttention (not linear/kernel attention) at
~70 cells per cluster; isolated photons to sidestep the 67% overlap problem for now; residual
on the physics baseline to combine engineered and learned features.
