# Research log — Week 1 (2026-06-10)

Goal: first look at the PicoCal matched-cluster dataset. Check it against its specification,
understand the inputs a model would see, and set up the classical baseline. Deliverables:
`docs/physics-primer.md`, the `picocal.data` loader with tests, and the exploration notebook
`notebooks/01_exploration_matched_clusters.ipynb`.

## What was done

- Set up the environment (uv venv, uproot/awkward) and extracted the full dataset:
  100 files, 199,538 matched clusters, 47 branches.
- Wrote `src/picocal/data/loader.py` (branch constants, jagged loading, cell mask, time
  cleaning, tokenization) with passing tests, including one integration test on the real files.
- Built and ran the exploration notebook.

## Findings (numbers are from the executed notebook)

1. Units: `sig_flux_eTot` is GeV (1–235, median 4.1). The spec sheet says MeV; the data and the
   mentors' example notebook agree on GeV.
2. The reconstructed energy is uncalibrated. Gain is about 967 raw units per GeV, measured on
   isolated photons above 4 GeV.
3. No padding: arrays are jagged and the `n*` branches equal the array length. About half of the
   window cells are silent (`energy == 0`). Informative cells per cluster: p50 = 71, p99 ≈ 200,
   max = 528.
4. Timing needs cleaning: uninitialised values (|t| up to ~1e37 ns) in silent cells and in ~20%
   of live cells. Cleaned times centre at ~86 ns, about twice the photon time of flight; the
   time reference is a mentor question. The undocumented `sig_flux_timing` branch matches d/c,
   so it is the truth arrival time at the ECAL.
5. Cell-time resolution improves with cell energy, from ~0.9 ns down to ~12 ps for the hottest
   cells, close to the SPIDER design figure of 15 ps. So the smearing looks realistic and a
   timing ablation is meaningful.
6. Up to ~13 photons per event, sometimes within 1 mm. Splits must be keyed on `(file, event)`.
7. 67% of photons are matched to a cluster that is also another photon's best match. Isolated
   photons (no sibling within 200 mm) show a flat, linear response; non-isolated ones inherit
   sibling energy, up to ×20 at 1 GeV. Energy regression on this dataset is also an overlap
   problem.
8. The front/back energy split falls with ln E as expected from shower physics, with a wide
   per-shower spread. At fixed energy the correlation between the response residual and the
   front fraction grows from ~0 below 50 GeV to +0.25 ± 0.05 at 170 GeV; a linear depth
   correction recovers 3–8% of the resolution there, while a global correction makes things
   worse.
9. The stored `x_cluster` is quantized (~140 distinct values per file) and sits 48 mm from the
   true entry point; a log-weighted (W0 = 4) centroid reaches 8.6 mm.
10. Local cell pitch derived from cell spacing reproduces the Run5 module-region layout, so the
    missing `cell_modType` can be replaced by a derived label.

## Baseline

Calibrated sum of cells, isolated photons (iso > 200 mm), dr < 100 mm, event-keyed split,
robust σ, fit restricted to E > 10 GeV:

- σ(E)/E = (11.3 ± 1.1)%/√E ⊕ (2.94 ± 0.14)%; |bias| below ~1.6% in the fit region.
- Below 10 GeV a diffuse contamination floor dominates (σ up to ~180% at 5 GeV).
- Contaminated regimes with the same calibration: non-isolated E > 4 GeV, p68 ≈ 7.6;
  all photons E < 4 GeV, p68 ≈ 29.

## Surprises / deviations from the plan

- The full dataset came as a single tar from CERNBox, so no download script was needed.
- This production lacks `cell_pitch`, `cell_modType` and `cell_rel_*`; the granularity label is
  derived from cell spacing instead.
- My first resolution fit was wrong: fitted over all energies without weights, it collapsed to
  a pure c/E term because the contaminated low-energy bins dominated. The corrected fit uses
  bootstrap uncertainties and is restricted to E > 10 GeV.
- I also first claimed the energy-weighted centroid does not beat `x_cluster` without checking;
  the numbers say the opposite (9.5 mm vs 48 mm). Corrected in section 9.

## Addendum (2026-06-12): with vs without minimum bias

Felipe shared a paired Drive sample — 5 files with minimum-bias overlay, 5 without (older
2024 productions) — so I compared them before going back to the 100-file set. Notebook:
`notebooks/02_minbias_comparison.ipynb`. What I learned:

- "Noise" here means minimum bias: real energy from the other pp collisions in the same
  bunch crossing (soft hadrons and π⁰ photons), not electronics noise.
- The overlay touches ~15% of cluster cells (vs <1% without), and the affected fraction
  grows toward the beam pipe.
- These files carry `cell_energies_*_orig`; orig minus current is a per-cell record of the
  removed overlay energy. If the training production keeps it, that is free per-cell
  supervision. Cells whose energy was removed entirely are not stored at all.
- The format differs from the 100-file set: only ~9 cluster-member cells per entry (no
  silent window cells, no seed branches), and the gain is production-specific
  (~2030 / ~1890 raw/GeV vs ~970).
- σ/E above 50 GeV: ≈12% with overlay vs ≈9% without. Indicative only — 5 files each and
  the productions differ in more than the overlay.
- Timing cannot tag the noise on this sample: overlay-only cells are missing and the kept
  cells' times match clean cells to 0.01 ns (the stored time seems to describe the cleaned
  deposit). Added mentor questions about whether the training production will keep noise
  cells with their raw times — the timing ablation depends on it.
